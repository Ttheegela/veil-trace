# The social side of evasion: a short summary

On the build day, ten web researchers each looked at one social force around sanctions, and two source
checkers re-read their sources. This page keeps **only the items the checkers confirmed**. Items that were
unverified, misattributed or sourced to a bare homepage are left out.

**The common thread.** Screening runs on names and paperwork. Each social force below either forges the
paperwork, removes it, or lowers the will to check it. These are context for the trade leads, not findings
about any company.

"Stake" means the source has an interest in the outcome; we say so where it applies.

## Forged paperwork

- A 2022 US indictment describes chips and microprocessors sent to Russia through a German front company,
  with end-use statements falsely claiming Malaysian customers. Source: the Wisconsin Project's summary of
  the charging documents. Confirmed.

## Dependence in hub countries

- Remittances equalled 31% of Kyrgyzstan's GDP and 27% of Tajikistan's in 2020. In 2021, 51% of remittances
  into Kazakhstan and 55% into Uzbekistan came from Russia. Source: International Organization for Migration
  (IOM). Confirmed.
- On 2026-02-26 a senior EU official told Kyrgyzstan to crack down on re-exports of EU goods to Russia
  (Reuters). Confirmed. The EU then used its anti-circumvention tool against Kyrgyzstan in April 2026 (see
  FINDINGS section 9).
- Why it matters: a crackdown that hits a whole region hurts families who depend on those remittances. That
  is an argument for targeting the few firms that carry a route, not the region.

## When help is refused

- UN Security Council Resolution 2664 (adopted 9 Dec 2022) created a standing humanitarian carve-out across
  nearly all UN sanctions regimes, after years of banks refusing lawful payments. Confirmed.

## Attacks on the signals screening relies on

- 113 ships flying a false flag moved an estimated EUR 4.7 billion of Russian oil in the first three quarters
  of 2025. Source: Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA), an energy think tank with an advocacy
  stake. Figures confirmed on its page.

## Corporate exits

- Yale's Chief Executive Leadership Institute has tracked the Russia status of more than 1,200 companies
  since February 2022. Confirmed.
- On 2026-03-31 OFAC published a sanctions advisory, "Guidance on Sham Transactions and Sanctions Evasion", on
  how to judge whether a sale of a Russian business is real or only on paper. Confirmed on OFAC's site.

## Alternative payment rails

- China's cross-border yuan clearing system (CIPS) had 1,683 participants by May 2025, up 10% on a year
  earlier (FXC Intelligence, a payments-industry analyst). Confirmed. Growth is real but small next to SWIFT.

## The story war

- The Jamestown Foundation (23 Feb 2024) documents Russian state-linked disinformation in African media about
  the war and sanctions. Confirmed.
- RUSI, a UK defence think tank, argued on 7 May 2024 that "Russia is winning the global information war".
  Confirmed.
- China's statements calling sanctions "illegal" are reported by the US-China Economic and Security Review
  Commission, a US government body with its own stake. Stake label added by the checker.

## Professional enablers

- ICIJ's "Cyprus Confidential" investigation (December 2023) documented how financial-services firms in Cyprus
  helped wealthy Russians after 2022. Confirmed.
- A roundup of enabler investigations dated 7 May 2024 comes from OCCRP's Global Anti-Corruption Consortium,
  not from RUSI as first reported. Attribution corrected.

## Online markets

- On 2022-06-23 a major Russian online marketplace launched a storefront for "parallel imports", goods brought
  in without the brand owner's consent (Reuters). Confirmed. Whether Telegram itself acts against evasion
  channels could not be verified.
