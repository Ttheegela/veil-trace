The Shape of the Problem

How sanctions are meant to work, how they are evaded, what anyone can see, and who pays. One idea per diagram; each should read in thirty seconds.

Diagram 1

The sanctions system

A list is a signal: it bites only when a bank, a distributor or a shipper recognises the name and refuses.

  • Governments (UN, US Treasury OFAC, US Commerce BIS, UK, EU) publish lists of names. The lists themselves stop nothing.
  • The stopping is done by private refusers: banks decline the payment, exporters and distributors decline the sale, shippers and insurers decline the cargo.
  • If a middle firm is new or unlisted, the refusers have no name to match, and goods and money flow through.

Diagram 2

The evasion playbook

Every trick breaks the match between a listed name and a transaction.

  • A part passes through several hands; each hand is a place to change a name, a label or an owner.
  • Inside the customs union there is no customs border, so the last leg into Russia has no customs check.
  • Oil uses the sea: ship-to-ship transfers and false flags hide where a cargo came from.

Diagram 3

What changes fast and what doesn't

Names last weeks, lists arrive after months, but the buyer and the part last years: track what persists.

  • On the Kyrgyz chip route the median firm was active 40 days, while the route itself kept flowing.
  • One buyer kept the same record from 2019 to 2025, and the same chip families kept arriving under new shipper names.
  • Lists arrived 15 to 23 months after a firm first appeared; most listed shippers had already stopped.

Diagram 4

Who can see which leg

Each source sees a few legs; the gaps between them are where evasion lives.

  • Our main trade data is mostly Russia's own import records, which thin out after late 2023.
  • Countries' own statistics show declared totals but no company names; registries and lists show names but not flows.
  • Some things are off the map entirely: ship positions, cloud access, payments and services.

Diagram 5

Our method

Agents find leads cheaply; a separate skeptic and an official page decide what can be shown.

  • Every number is re-derived from saved raw data by a checker that did not produce it.
  • Listing dates and list entries come from official pages, never from a data vendor's flag alone.
  • Unlisted companies stay agency-only. Today the checks caught four errors before they reached a slide.

Diagram 6

Who pays, who gains

The detour pays the middle, costs the careful, and its harm lands on people far from the deal.

  • Russian buyers pay middlemen for the detour; the middle firms take a margin, and a new firm is cheap to open.
  • Banks and makers carry the risk of fines, so the cheap answer is to refuse whole regions.
  • The costs fall on civilians hit by the weapons, and on honest traders and families caught by clumsy refusals.

Figures come from the team's verified log of 25 September 2026 (sponsor trade data re-checked by an independent verifier, UN Comtrade aggregate rows, official listing pages) and cited reports (CREA on false-flag tankers).
